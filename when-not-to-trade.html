When Not to Trade, and Where to Put SL & TP

The trades you skip protect your account as much as the trades you take. Then, for the ones you do take, the stop loss and take profit must come from structure, not from hope.

  • When not to trade
  • Stop loss
  • Take profit
  • Risk to reward

1When not to trade

📓 From your notes

Only trade when there is high probability setup. Remember, best time to trade is when a setup meets your criteria.

⚖️

1. Price in the middle

Equilibrium of the range: no discount, no premium, no edge.

🌫

2. Slow, choppy price action

No clear bias. Overlapping candles, no clean breaks. Wait for clarity.

📅

3. Mondays & Fridays

Monday often builds the weekly range without direction; Friday has profit taking and position squaring before the weekend.

🎄

4. December

Holiday season: institutions close books, liquidity thins and moves get random.

📰

5. Right before and after high impact news

Spreads widen, slippage, wild spikes through both sides. Wait for the dust to settle (for example 30 minutes).

😤

6. When not in the correct mood

Tired, angry, distracted or euphoric. Your state affects your execution.

📉

7. Losing streaks

After a set number of losses, stop for the day or week. Review, do not revenge.

🗓

8. Last day of the month

Month-end flows and rebalancing by funds can produce moves unrelated to structure.

💡 These are personal rules

Points 3, 4 and 8 are filters many traders use, not laws of the market. Check them against your own journal: if your Mondays are fine, keep trading them. The principle behind all eight is the same: only trade when conditions favour clean, readable structure.

⚠️ Use an economic calendar

Check a calendar (for example Forex Factory or your broker's) every morning for red-folder events: interest rate decisions, CPI, Non-Farm Payrolls, central bank speeches.

2Where to place the stop loss

📓 From your notes

Q16: Where to place SL & TP. Ans: At the consolidation or previous stop of price. At your decided TP. At your RR.

A stop loss belongs at the price that proves your idea wrong, not at a random number of pips.

  • Buying from a zone/OB: just below the zone, or below the consolidation / swing low that price stopped at.
  • Selling from a zone/OB: just above the zone, or above the swing high.
  • Add a small buffer (spread + a few pips) so a minor sweep does not take you out.
  • Never move it further away once the trade is live. You may move it to break-even after a clear new BOS in your favour.

3Where to place the take profit

  • At the next liquidity: previous high/low, yesterday's high/low, equal highs/lows.
  • Before the opposing zone: supply when buying, demand when selling.
  • At an FVG in your direction.
  • At your planned RR: decide the TP before entering and let the trade reach it. Place TP slightly before the level so you actually get filled.

4Risk to reward (RR)

RR = (Take profit − Entry) ÷ (Entry − Stop loss) // for a buy example: entry 1.0850, SL 1.0842 (8 pips), TP 1.0874 (24 pips) → RR = 24 ÷ 8 = 1 : 3

RR decides how often you need to win. With 1 : 3, you can lose 3 trades out of 4 and still break even. See the chart in Risk Management.

✅ Key takeaways
  • Skip mid-range, choppy, news, tilted-mood and losing-streak conditions.
  • Stop loss = where the idea is invalid (beyond the zone or consolidation), plus a buffer.
  • Take profit = next liquidity, opposing zone or FVG, decided before entry.
  • Know your RR before you click.
