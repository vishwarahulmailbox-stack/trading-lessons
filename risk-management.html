Risk Management

You can be wrong half the time and still grow an account, but only if each loss is small and controlled. This lesson turns the principles in your notes into numbers you can calculate and see.

1The five principles

📓 From your notes

Principles: 1) Protect your capital first 2) Define your risk before every trade 3) Keep risk consistent 4) Use guide rail to control yourself 5) Think in probabilities, not certainty.

1. Protect capital first

No capital, no trading. Survival comes before growth.

2. Define risk before every trade

You know the stop and the dollar amount before you enter.

3. Keep risk consistent

Same % on every trade. Otherwise one big loss erases ten small wins.

4. Use guardrails

Hard limits that stop you even when emotions say "one more".

5. Probabilities, not certainty

Any single trade can lose. Your edge only shows over many trades.

2The five risk rules

📓 From your notes

Risk rules: 1) Risk a fixed % per trade 2) Set a max daily trade 3) Respect your max drawdown 4) Limit total trade per day 5) Only risk on A+ setup.

3Position size calculator

"Risk a fixed %" only works if the position size changes with the stop distance. A wide stop means a smaller position; a tight stop allows a bigger one. The dollar risk stays the same.

Money at risk = Account × Risk % Position (lots) = Money at risk ÷ (Stop loss in pips × Pip value per lot)

5Win rate vs risk to reward

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + RR) Expectancy (in R) = (Win rate × RR) − (Loss rate × 1)

✅ Key takeaways
  • Risk a small, fixed % per trade and size the position from the stop distance.
  • Losses are asymmetric: protect capital first.
  • Higher RR lowers the win rate you need; expectancy must be positive.
  • Guardrails (daily loss, max trades, max drawdown) protect you from yourself.
