Top Down Analysis

Look at multiple timeframes to form a single story. The higher timeframe gives you the bigger story, the lower timeframe gives you precision. Never the other way round.

  • The idea
  • Timeframe ladder
  • HTF / MTF / LTF
  • Stacks by style
  • Worked example

1One story, many zoom levels

📓 From your notes

Look multiple TF to form single story. HTF will give bigger story, LTF will give precision. Going down in timeframe is like zoom in to price action. HTF run is a LTF trend.

🗺 Analogy

It is like Google Maps. The country view (HTF) tells you which direction the city is. The city view (MTF) shows the neighbourhood you are heading to. Street view (LTF) shows the exact door. Starting at street view, you would walk confidently in the wrong direction.

2The timeframe ladder

📓 From your notes

HTF, more powerful the information. Like, higher TF is higher confidence. M > W > D > 4H > 1H > 15M > 5M.

3The job of each timeframe

HTF: build the narrative and trend direction

  1. Is the market bullish, bearish or ranging?From market structure.
  2. Where is price relative to key supply or demand?Approaching a major zone changes everything.
  3. Is price in premium or discount?Buy only in discount, sell only in premium. See Premium & Discount.
  4. Where is liquidity nearby?Old highs, old lows, equal levels: the next magnet.
  5. Are we approaching an order block, FVG or major zone?That is where the story will make its next decision.

MTF: immediate bias plus Point of Interest (POI)

A POI is an area where you are willing to enter: an order block, an FVG, a supply/demand zone or a flip zone. After the HTF gives direction, the MTF helps you find:

  • The dealing zone: the current swing range you are trading inside.
  • The exact zones inside it (the POIs).
  • Internal structure of the pullback.
  • Whether price is approaching your area properly: calmly in a pullback, not in a violent expansion straight through it.

LTF: trade execution inside the MTF POI

  • Waiting for price to enter your area.
  • Seeing if the model forms (for example a sweep then CHoCH).
  • Watching for confirmation.
  • Refining entry and stop loss: a small LTF zone inside the big MTF zone gives a much tighter stop.
📓 From your notes

Always do analysis from TOP to bottom [HTF to LTF]. I will never [NEVER] look into LTF until price has moved to my Point of Interest in MTF.

⚠️ Why the NEVER rule exists

The 1M and 5M charts always show something: a little break, a little pattern. Looking at them early creates fake signals and boredom trades. The LTF is only allowed to speak once price is inside your MTF POI.

5Worked example: a full top down read

  1. HTFDaily: "Buyers in control, price is cheap."HH and HL, price has pulled back below 50% of the last leg. Bias: look for buys only. Target: the daily high (liquidity).
  2. MTF1H: "This is where I want to buy."Inside the daily discount there is a 1H order block with an FVG above it. Mark it. Set an alert. Close the charts.
  3. LTF5M: "Now, and only now."Price reaches the 1H POI. On the 5M it sweeps a small low and then breaks the last lower high (CHoCH). Enter on the pullback, stop below the sweep low.
✅ Key takeaways
  • Always analyse from the top down. Higher timeframe = higher confidence.
  • HTF: who is in control. MTF: where (POI). LTF: when (confirmation).
  • Never open the LTF until price reaches your MTF POI.
  • LTF entries inside HTF zones give tight stops and large risk to reward.
