Liquidity

"If you can't spot the liquidity, then you are most likely the liquidity." This lesson shows you where the stop orders hide, how big players hunt them, and how to use that as both a warning and a target.

1What is liquidity?

📓 From your notes

If you can't spot the liquidity then you are most likely liquidity. At these levels there are stop losses + stop orders ⇒ FUEL. Price cannot move without liquidity sweep.

In this context, liquidity means clusters of resting orders, mainly stop losses and pending stop orders, sitting at predictable prices. A fund that wants to sell 1 billion needs 1 billion of buy orders to sell into. Where does it find them? Above obvious highs, where sellers keep their stop losses (a stop on a short is a buy order) and breakout traders keep their buy-stop entries.

2Buy-side and sell-side liquidity

Buy-side liquidity (BSL)

Sits above highs. Stop losses of short sellers and buy-stop orders of breakout buyers. When triggered they are buy orders, perfect for big players who want to sell.

Sell-side liquidity (SSL)

Sits below lows. Stop losses of buyers and sell-stop orders of breakout sellers. When triggered they are sell orders, perfect for big players who want to buy.

3The 5 patterns where liquidity hides

PatternWhy stops gather there
1. Highs / lowsEvery swing point is a natural place for a stop.
2. Strong highs / lowsThe swing that caused a BOS; everyone in the new trend hides their stop beyond it.
3. Equal highs / lowsRetail sees "double top / double bottom" and "strong support": stacked stops. A favourite target.
4. TrendlinesTraders buy each touch and put stops just under the line. Trendline liquidity builds with every touch.
5. Support & resistanceTextbook levels that every beginner can see, with textbook stops right behind them.

4The liquidity sweep (stop hunt)

How to tell a sweep from a real breakout:

Liquidity sweepReal breakout (BOS)
Candle closeWick through the level, closes back insideBody closes beyond the level
Follow-throughQuick rejection the other wayContinues, then pulls back to retest
Next clueLTF CHoCH against the spike, often an FVGNew higher structure holds
LocationOften into an HTF zone in premium/discountOften from discount in trend direction
🛠 Two nuances on your notes

"Price cannot move without liquidity sweep": a good way to think, but strictly speaking price moves whenever orders are matched. The point is that the big moves usually start right after a pool of liquidity has been taken.
"Trust liquidity sweep, always form a FVG": a strong sweep is usually followed by a displacement that leaves an FVG, and that FVG is your confirmation. But not every sweep does it. If no FVG and no CHoCH appear, do not trust the sweep yet.

5Inducement: traps for retail traders

📓 From your notes

Inducement: traps to entice retail trader, so to generate liquidity.

The lesson: when you see an easy, obvious entry just above your real zone, be suspicious. Let price take the inducement first. The best entries often come after the obvious one has failed.

6Using liquidity: trap to avoid, target to hit

As a warning

Never put your stop exactly where everyone else does (just below an obvious low). Give it room beyond the zone, or wait for the sweep before you enter.

As an entry signal

Sweep of a level into your POI + LTF CHoCH + FVG = classic smart money entry.

As a target

The opposite pool of liquidity is where price is drawn next. Previous day high/low and equal highs/lows make great take profit levels.

✅ Key takeaways
  • Liquidity = clusters of stop and pending orders above highs (BSL) and below lows (SSL).
  • Obvious levels (equal highs/lows, trendlines, S/R) hold the most liquidity.
  • A sweep wicks through and closes back; a breakout closes through and holds.
  • Beware inducement just before your real POI. Use opposite liquidity as your target.
