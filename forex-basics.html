1What is forex?

📓 From your notes

Global marketplace where the world's currencies exchange hands at mutually agreed rate. Open: 24 × 7 (Mon to Fri). Conversion from one to another.

Forex (FX, foreign exchange) is the largest financial market in the world. There is no single building or exchange: it is a network of banks, brokers and institutions trading currencies with each other electronically. When a company in Japan pays a supplier in Germany, when a tourist changes money, when a fund buys US shares, currency is exchanged.

🛠 Small correction

The market is open 24 hours a day, 5 days a week (24/5), not 24 × 7. It opens Sunday evening New York time when Sydney and Tokyo come online, and closes Friday evening New York time. Weekends are closed, which is why you sometimes see a "gap" between Friday's close and Monday's open.

2Reading a currency pair

Currencies are always quoted against each other, in pairs. The price answers one question: how many units of the quote currency do I need to get 1 unit of the base currency?

3What buying and selling really mean

📓 From your notes

If Buy EUR/USD then you make profit if EUR ↑ & USD ↓. If sell EUR/USD then you make profit if EUR ↓ & USD ↑. If one goes up then other has to go ↓. All cannot go up at same time.

Buying a pair means you buy the base and sell the quote at the same time. Selling a pair means you sell the base and buy the quote. A pair is a ratio, so it is impossible for both currencies to rise against each other: if EUR strengthens versus USD, USD has by definition weakened versus EUR.

4Pips and lots (not in the notes, but you need them)

Your trading styles notes measure targets in pips, so let's define them properly.

Pip

The standard unit of price movement. For most pairs it is the 4th decimal: 0.0001. EUR/USD moving from 1.35361 to 1.35461 is +10 pips.

JPY pairs

For pairs quoted in yen (USD/JPY, GBP/JPY) a pip is the 2nd decimal: 0.01. 150.20 to 150.70 is 50 pips.

Lot sizes

1 standard lot = 100,000 units of the base currency. Mini = 10,000. Micro = 1,000.

Pip value

For pairs quoted in USD (EUR/USD, GBP/USD), 1 pip on a standard lot ≈ $10, mini ≈ $1, micro ≈ $0.10.

Pip value is what links your stop loss to real money. You will use it in the position size calculator.

5Who trades forex?

📓 From your notes

Big banks, large financial institutes, governments & companies.

🏛

Central banks & governments

Set interest rates and sometimes intervene directly. Their decisions move currencies for months.

🏦

Big banks (interbank)

The core of the market. They trade with each other and make prices for everyone else.

📈

Funds & institutions

Hedge funds, pension funds, asset managers. Huge orders that need liquidity: the "smart money" of these lessons.

🏭

Companies

Exporters and importers converting revenue and paying suppliers.

🧑‍💻

Retail traders (us)

A small slice of volume. We cannot move price, so we learn to follow the footprints of those who can.

This is the reason behind every concept in this course. Institutions leave footprints (zones, order blocks, imbalances, liquidity sweeps) because their orders are too big to hide. We trade by reading those footprints.

6Need or build the 3 Ms

Method

A written, repeatable set of rules: what you trade, when, why, and exactly how you enter and exit. Lessons 5 to 15 build this.

Money management

How much you risk per trade, daily limits, maximum drawdown. Covered in Risk Management.

Mindset

Following the rules when it is uncomfortable. Covered in Beliefs & Mindset.

7Major pairs and the best time to trade

📓 From your notes

Major pairs: anything with USD. EUR/USD, GBP/USD, JPY/USD. Best time: London & NY session.

🛠 Small correction

The yen pair is quoted the other way round: USD/JPY (how many yen for one dollar), not JPY/USD. Also, "major" usually means one of these seven most traded USD pairs:

London and New York are the best sessions because that is when the big banks are at their desks and the most liquidity is available. The overlap of the two is usually the busiest part of the day. Lesson 14, Kill Zones, gives you the exact hours.

✅ Key takeaways
  • A pair's price = quote currency needed for 1 unit of base currency.
  • Buy = you expect the base to strengthen. Sell = you expect it to weaken.
  • A pip is 0.0001 (0.01 for JPY pairs). 1 standard lot ≈ $10 per pip on USD-quoted pairs.
  • Institutions move the market. Build the 3 Ms: Method, Money management, Mindset.
