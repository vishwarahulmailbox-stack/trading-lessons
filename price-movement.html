How Price Moves

Before patterns, zones or indicators, you need one mental model: price moves because orders meet orders. Liquidity is the fuel, and the market is forever swinging between balance and imbalance.

1Liquidity is the fuel

📓 From your notes

Q1: How does the price move? Ans: Liquidity [FUEL]. Price tries to balance itself. Volume of buyers vs sellers.

Every trade needs two sides. If you want to buy 1 lot of EUR/USD, somebody has to sell you 1 lot. Liquidity simply means the orders that are available to trade against: limit orders waiting in the market, and stop orders waiting to be triggered.

A small trader can always find a counterparty. A bank that wants to buy 2 billion euros cannot just click "buy": there are not enough sellers at one price. It has to go and find sell orders, and the act of finding them moves price.

🚗 Analogy

Think of price as a car and liquidity as petrol stations. The car only drives where it can refuel. Big players steer price toward levels where lots of orders are resting (old highs, old lows, obvious support and resistance) because that is where they can fill their huge positions.

3Balance and imbalance

The market lives in a loop. Your notes draw it as a triangle: Efficient market → Imbalance (Supply > Demand or Demand > Supply) → Look for balance.

On a chart this looks like boxes (ranges) joined by fast diagonal moves:

📓 From your notes

The market always seeks Fair Value Gap. Enter in balance state of price & capture the imbalance.

Two practical ideas come out of this:

  • Fast moves leave unfinished business. During an imbalance price moved so quickly that only one side traded. Those areas (later called Fair Value Gaps) tend to be revisited, as price "rebalances".
  • Get in during balance, get paid during imbalance. The best entries are taken while price is quiet (a range, a pullback into a zone). The profit comes from the expansion that follows.

4The 4 stages of price

Zoom into any chart and you will see the same four phases repeating on every timeframe.

StageWhat it looks likeWhat it meansWhat you do
1. ExpansionBig candles, strong move in one directionImbalance: one side is in full controlDo not chase. Note where it started (that is a zone).
2. PullbackTemporary retracement against the moveProfit taking, the market "breathes"Look for entries in the direction of the expansion, ideally in discount.
3. ConsolidationCompression, pause, small candles in a boxBalance. Orders are building up on both sidesMark the range high and low: liquidity sits just outside them.
4. Reversal / ContinuationPrice breaks out of the boxThe decision after the pauseWait for a break of structure to tell you which way, then trade with it.
💡 Going deeper

The stages are fractal. A 4H pullback may contain a full expansion, pullback, consolidation sequence on the 15M chart. That is why top down analysis matters: you always need to know which stage the higher timeframe is in.

5Ask yourself, every time you open a chart

📓 From your notes

Don't trade EGO | PRIDE, only trade PRICE.

  1. Is price expanding, pulling back or consolidating?Name the stage before anything else.
  2. Who is in control: buyers or sellers?Higher highs and higher lows say buyers; lower highs and lower lows say sellers.
  3. Where are the obvious highs and lows?If they are obvious to you, they are obvious to everyone. That is where stops (liquidity) sit.
  4. Where might price be drawing toward?The next pool of liquidity or an unfilled imbalance is the likely magnet.
  5. Is the market continuing, or preparing to shift?Watch for failed highs or lows and a change of character.
✅ Key takeaways
  • Price moves to find orders. Liquidity is the fuel.
  • The market alternates between balance (ranges) and imbalance (expansions).
  • Four stages repeat on every timeframe: expansion, pullback, consolidation, decision.
  • Enter during balance or pullbacks, profit from the imbalance. Trade price, not your ego.
