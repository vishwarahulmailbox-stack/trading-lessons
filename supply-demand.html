Institutional Supply & Demand Zones

Big players cannot fill their whole order at once. They build positions in quiet areas, then price explodes away. Those launch pads are supply and demand zones, and price often comes back to them.

  • Why they work

  • Range vs pivot

1Supply zones and demand zones

📓 From your notes

1) Supply zone: created after price aggressively moves out of the consolidation to downside. 2) Demand zone: created after price aggressively moves out of the consolidation to upward.

Demand zone

A base (small consolidation) followed by a strong rally. Buyers were accumulating. Look to buy when price returns.

Supply zone

A base followed by a strong drop. Sellers were distributing. Look to sell when price returns.

2Why price returns to zones

Imagine a fund that wants to buy 500 million EUR/USD. It buys a chunk in the base, but its buying pushes price up so fast that only part of the order gets filled. The rest of the order is still waiting at those prices. When price comes back, those unfilled orders (and new ones from traders who know the level) push price away again.

💡 The connection to lesson 1

The base is a balance, the rally is an imbalance. Supply and demand zones are simply where balance turned into imbalance.

3How to spot a zone

📓 From your notes

Look for imbalance in price to spot supply & demand zone. The stronger the move away from the zone the important that zone usually is.

  1. 1Find the explosive moveBig candles, little overlap, often leaving a fair value gap.
  2. 2Look left, to where it startedThe small candles or single candle just before the explosion: that is the base.
  3. 3Draw the zoneUsing the range method or the pivot method (next section).
  4. 4Extend it to the rightKeep it on the chart until price comes back.

4Two methods to draw a zone

📓 From your notes

2 Methods: 1) Range (whole consolidation) 2) Pivot (a candle caused the break).

Range methodPivot method
What you boxThe whole consolidationOne candle: the one that caused the break
Zone sizeWiderTighter
Chance price reaches itHigherLower (may be missed)
Stop lossBiggerSmaller, so better risk to reward
Best forHTF zones, beginnersRefined entries, often on MTF / LTF

5Range method: wait for 50% of the zone

📓 From your notes

If price mitigates the range zone, wait until it's mitigated 50% of the zone. 50% can be identified by Discount.

Mitigated means price has returned into the zone and the waiting orders have been (at least partly) used. A wide zone's first edge is the least reliable part. The middle of the zone is the "discount of the zone" when buying (or the premium of a supply zone when selling), the same logic as premium and discount, applied to the zone itself.

6Grading a zone: A+ or skip?

✅ Key takeaways
  • A zone is the base right before an aggressive move. Demand = before a rally, supply = before a drop.
  • The stronger the move away, the more important the zone.
  • Range method: wider, safer fill, bigger stop. Pivot method: tight, great RR, can be missed.
  • On wide range zones, wait for price to reach 50% of the zone.
