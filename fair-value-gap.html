Fair Value Gaps (FVG)

A fair value gap is the visible footprint of an imbalance: a spot where price moved so fast that only one side traded. Markets tend to come back and rebalance it.

  • What it is
  • Find one, step by step
  • Why price returns
  • How to use it
  • Mistakes

1What is a fair value gap?

📓 From your notes

Also called as Imbalance or Inefficiency. We expect market to pull back at the FVG range. Alone FVG is not complete for setup, should be combined with other parameters.

An FVG is a three candle pattern. The middle candle is big and aggressive. If the wick of candle 1 and the wick of candle 3 do not overlap, the empty space between them is the fair value gap. In that space, price moved in one direction only: buyers (or sellers) never got a chance to trade the other way.

3Why price comes back

Remember lesson 1: the market always seeks balance. Inside an FVG, there was no two-sided trading, so the price there is not yet "fair". Price often returns to rebalance: to let the other side trade there and to fill orders that were missed in the rush.

💡 Consequent encroachment (CE)

The 50% level of an FVG is called consequent encroachment. Strong FVGs often hold at or above their CE. If price closes fully through the FVG, it has failed, and that is information too (the imbalance has been rejected).

5Common mistakes

Trading every gap

Lower timeframes are full of FVGs. Without structure and context most of them fail.

Using wicks vs bodies inconsistently

Measure from wick to wick (candle 1 wick, candle 3 wick). Be consistent.

Ignoring already-filled gaps

Once price has traded through the whole gap, it is balanced. It no longer has the same pull.

✅ Key takeaways
  • FVG = 3 candles where wicks of candle 1 and 3 do not overlap.
  • It shows an imbalance; price tends to return to rebalance it.
  • The 50% level (CE) is a key reaction point.
  • An FVG needs confluence: trend, BOS, discount/premium, OB, liquidity sweep.
