Order Blocks (OB)

An order block is the last opposite candle before a sharp move that breaks structure. It marks where big money entered, and where it often re-enters. Your notes give a strict 5 rule test for a valid one.

1What is an order block?

📓 From your notes

Order Block are area, where Big Money enter and Re-Enter (chances to enter again at same price). Order Block is valid until it is not mitigated.

A bullish order block is the last bearish candle before a sharp, substantial rally. Why that candle? Institutions buying in size often push price down first (to sweep sell-side liquidity and get filled at good prices), then their buying launches price up. The down candle is where those orders sat. When price returns, the remaining orders defend the level.

2Build one step by step

3The formation rules

📓 From your notes

Rule: 1) Pivot candle must take out previous candle's low. 2) Gap or Imbalance created. 3) Sharp & substantial upward movement. 4) Order Block is formed at the last bearish candle before sharp upward movement. Vice versa for bearish move.

  1. The pivot candle takes out the previous candle's low.That small sweep grabs sell-side liquidity: stops of early buyers. Big money fills against them.
  2. A gap or imbalance (FVG) is created.Proof that the move was aggressive and one-sided.
  3. The move is sharp and substantial.Not a slow grind. Displacement: large candles covering a lot of distance.
  4. The OB is the last bearish candle before that move.Mark it from its high to its low (or open to low for a tighter version).

4The FVG's base candle is the order block

📓 From your notes

FVG's base candle is the Order Block zone.

This links lessons 10 and 11. Find a good FVG, look at the candle before the big middle candle (candle 1 of the FVG). If that candle is opposite in colour, it is the order block. Together they form a strong POI: price often taps the FVG, then the OB below it.

5Rule for a valid OB: all five are needed

📓 From your notes

Rule for valid OB [All needed]: 1) Should have FVG 2) It needs to break structure 3) Order Block should be in Discount [BUY] or Premium [SELL] of the swing high & swing low 4) Wait until the price comes back to Order Block zone 5) Look for the market structure [very important].

⚠️ "Valid until mitigated"

Once price has come back into the OB and reacted, most of its orders are used. A second or third return is much weaker. Treat a fully mitigated OB as finished, and if price closes straight through it, the OB has failed: it may even become a flip zone for the other side.

✅ Key takeaways
  • Bullish OB = last bearish candle before a sharp, structure-breaking rally (bearish = mirror).
  • Good OBs sweep a little liquidity, leave an FVG and break structure.
  • All five validity rules are needed, including discount/premium and market structure.
  • An OB is valid until it is mitigated.
